**You need travel insurance for Zimbabwe, and it must include medical evacuation.** Insurance isn't an entry requirement, but healthcare outside the main cities is limited, private clinics expect payment before they treat you, and serious cases are flown to South Africa. Your policy also needs to cover the activities you plan, such as walking safaris, rafting or the Victoria Falls bungee.

**Written by [Bryan Fawcett](/about-this-guide).**

<Note>
  **Last reviewed: 21 October 2026** against the UK FCDO's Zimbabwe travel advice and its guidance on foreign travel insurance, and the US CDC's Zimbabwe traveller page. We don't recommend individual insurers or quote prices, because cover and premiums depend on your age, health, home country and trip. Compare policies and read the wording before you buy.
</Note>

## Why insurance matters in Zimbabwe

- **Healthcare is limited.** The FCDO says healthcare is variable and can be very poor outside the major cities, with shortages of medicines and staff.
- **You pay first.** Private clinics won't treat patients until they pay, and often want large amounts of cash before admitting emergency cases, even if you're insured. Some medical providers only accept US dollars in cash.
- **Evacuation is expensive.** The FCDO warns that medical costs, particularly for evacuation, can be high. From a remote camp, that can mean a light aircraft or helicopter to Harare, then an air ambulance to South Africa.
- **Safaris are prepaid.** Lodges usually take deposits months in advance, and cancellation terms can be strict.

## What your policy should cover

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion icon="helicopter" title="Medical treatment and evacuation (essential)">
    - Emergency medical and hospital treatment.
    - **Medical evacuation**, including air ambulance from a remote area to Harare or South Africa, and repatriation home.
    - A **24-hour assistance line** that can guarantee payment to a clinic or arrange evacuation.
    - Check whether ambulance and emergency transport costs are covered, because the FCDO notes they're often charged separately.

    Ask your insurer how they work with Zimbabwe's private medical rescue services, **MARS** and **ACE Air & Ambulance**. Many safari lodges subscribe to one of them. See [emergency numbers](/essentials/travel-advice#emergency-numbers-in-zimbabwe).

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="person-hiking" title="Activities">
    The FCDO advises making sure your insurance covers all the activities you plan in Zimbabwe, and notes that walking and canoe safaris carry risks. Activities that standard policies often exclude, or cover only with an add-on, include:

    - White-water rafting on the Zambezi
    - Bungee jumping, gorge swings and zip lines at Victoria Falls
    - Microlight and helicopter flights
    - Walking safaris and canoe safaris
    - Self-drive safaris and off-road driving
    - Hiking and scrambling in the Eastern Highlands

    Name each activity when you get a quote and get the answer in writing.

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="calendar-xmark" title="Cancellation and curtailment">
    Cover for cancelling or cutting short your trip protects prepaid lodge, flight and activity costs. Set the cancellation limit to at least the non-refundable value of your trip, and check what counts as a valid reason to cancel.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="suitcase" title="Baggage, cash and cameras">
    Check single-item limits for cameras, lenses, binoculars and phones. If your kit is worth more than the limit, consider separate gadget or camera insurance. Cash cover is usually small.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="plane-circle-exclamation" title="Delays and missed connections">
    Regional flights are sometimes delayed or cancelled. Cover for delays and missed connections helps if you're connecting through Johannesburg, Addis Ababa, Nairobi or Dubai. See [transportation](/essentials/transportation).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion icon="car" title="Car hire">
    Travel insurance doesn't usually cover damage to a hire car. Check the hire company's insurance and excess, and whether gravel roads, national parks and border crossings are covered.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Common exclusions

The FCDO's guidance on travel insurance highlights these:

- **Undeclared medical conditions.** Tell your insurer about every existing condition, including mental health conditions and pending tests or treatment. Failing to declare something may invalidate your policy.
- **Alcohol and drugs.** Most policies don't cover events after excessive drinking.
- **Travel against official advice.** If your government advises against travel to an area, your insurance may be invalid. None of the US, UK or Australian governments currently advise against travel to any part of Zimbabwe. See our [travel advice](/essentials/travel-advice).
- **Unlisted activities.** Anything not named in the policy may be excluded.

## Malaria and other health risks

- Malaria treatment is normally covered as an illness, but antimalarial tablets bought before you travel are not.
- Take your antimalarials as prescribed and keep the receipts. Check your policy's wording on illness from not following medical advice.
- See [health and safety](/essentials/health-and-safety) for vaccines and malaria advice.

## When to buy

Buy your policy as soon as you've booked, so cancellation cover starts straight away. Some benefits, such as cover for pre-existing conditions or "cancel for any reason" add-ons sold in some countries, must be bought within a set time of your first payment.

## In an emergency

1. **Call your insurer's assistance line** as early as you can. They can guarantee payment, approve treatment and arrange evacuation.
2. Ask your lodge or operator for help. They usually know the nearest clinic and rescue service.
3. Keep every receipt and medical report, and get a police report for theft.
4. Carry a credit card and access to emergency funds for upfront payments.

## Checklist

- Medical treatment and evacuation, including repatriation
- A 24-hour assistance line, saved in your phone
- Every activity you plan, named in writing
- Cancellation cover at least equal to your non-refundable costs
- All medical conditions declared
- Policy documents saved offline and shared with someone at home

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Is travel insurance required to enter Zimbabwe?">
    No, it isn't an entry requirement. But the UK FCDO tells travellers to have appropriate insurance for local treatment and medical evacuation, and some lodges and operators ask for your policy details.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does my health insurance at home cover me?">
    Usually not fully, and most home health plans don't cover medical evacuation abroad. Check with your provider, and buy travel insurance to fill the gaps.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will my credit card's travel insurance be enough?">
    Sometimes, but card cover often has low medical limits and excludes adventure activities. Read the policy wording and compare it with the checklist above.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does insurance cover the Victoria Falls bungee and rafting?">
    Only if the policy says so. Many standard policies exclude them or need an add-on. Ask before you buy and get written confirmation.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
