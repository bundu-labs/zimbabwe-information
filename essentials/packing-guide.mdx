Smart packing for Zimbabwe balances practicality with climate and activity needs. This guide helps you pack for safari, adventure activities and the different seasons while staying within baggage limits.

## Baggage limits

**Light aircraft (charter flights to safari camps):**

- **Weight:** often 15–20kg per person, sometimes including hand luggage and camera bags. Your operator will tell you the exact limit
- **Bag type:** soft-sided bags only (duffels or backpacks), because hard cases don't fit in small aircraft holds
- **Applies to:** fly-in safaris, for example to Mana Pools, Matusadona or Hwange's private airstrips. If you drive or transfer by road, standard limits apply

**Scheduled flights:**

- Each airline sets its own allowance. Check your ticket, especially for regional and domestic flights, which can be lower than long-haul allowances

**If you've packed too much:**

- Ask your hotel in Victoria Falls or Harare, or your operator, to store extra luggage
- Take only safari essentials to bush camps
- Collect your bags after the safari

## Packing by season

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Cool and dry (May–Aug)">
    **Climate:**
    - Days: about 21–29°C
    - Nights: about 5–14°C, coldest inland in June and July
    - Almost no rain
    - Dusty conditions

    **Pack:**
    - ✅ Warm layers (fleece, down jacket)
    - ✅ Beanie/warm hat for early game drives
    - ✅ Long trousers and long sleeves (safari colours)
    - ✅ Sunglasses and sunscreen (harsh sun)
    - ✅ Lip balm (dry conditions)
    - ✅ Moisturiser
    - ❌ Rain gear (rarely needed, except for spray at Victoria Falls)
    - ❌ Heavy winter coat (layers better)

    **Why Cold?**
    Open safari vehicles + wind chill + dawn temperatures = very cold mornings!

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Hot and dry (Sep–Oct)">
    **Climate:**
    - Days: up to 33–35°C in the low-lying parks, such as Victoria Falls, Kariba and Mana Pools
    - Nights: mild, about 12–17°C at Victoria Falls
    - Humidity builds before the rains
    - Intense sun

    **Pack:**
    - ✅ Lightweight, breathable fabrics
    - ✅ Sun hat with wide brim
    - ✅ High SPF sunscreen
    - ✅ Sunglasses (essential)
    - ✅ Insect repellent (more mosquitoes)
    - ✅ Light layers for evening
    - ✅ Rehydration salts
    - ❌ Heavy clothing
    - ❌ Dark colours (absorb heat)

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Rains (Nov–Apr)">
    **Climate:**
    - Days: warm to hot, about 28–32°C in the low-lying areas
    - Nights: about 17–18°C at Victoria Falls and Hwange
    - Afternoon thunderstorms, heaviest from December to February
    - High humidity

    **Pack:**
    - ✅ Light rain jacket (breathable)
    - ✅ Waterproof bag covers
    - ✅ Quick-dry clothing
    - ✅ Extra socks (things stay damp)
    - ✅ Sandals/water-resistant shoes
    - ✅ Insect repellent (peak malaria season)
    - ✅ Zip-lock bags (protect electronics)
    - ❌ Cotton clothing (takes forever to dry)

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Documents

**Carry in Person:**

- ☐ Passport (valid for at least 6 months, with blank pages)
- ☐ E-visa approval, or US dollars in cash for a visa on arrival (see [visas and entry](/essentials/visas-and-entry))
- ☐ QR code from the online arrival form
- ☐ Travel insurance documents
- ☐ Yellow fever certificate, only if you're arriving from a country with yellow fever risk (see [health and safety](/essentials/health-and-safety))
- ☐ Flight tickets/confirmations
- ☐ Accommodation bookings
- ☐ Driving licence and International Driving Permit (if self-driving)
- ☐ Credit cards (Visa/Mastercard best)
- ☐ US dollars in cash, in small, clean notes

**Photocopies/Digital Backups:**

- Passport photo page
- Visa
- Travel insurance policy
- Credit card numbers (separate from cards)
- Emergency contacts
- Store in email/cloud for access anywhere

## Clothing

### Safari clothing

**Colours:**

- **Best:** Khaki, tan, olive, brown, beige, sage green
- **Avoid:** bright colours (they stand out to animals), white (shows dirt immediately), and dark blue or black, which attract tsetse flies
- **Never:** camouflage or military-style clothing. It's illegal for civilians in Zimbabwe, including children's clothing and bags

**What to Pack:**

- ☐ 3-4 long-sleeve shirts (sun protection, insect protection)
- ☐ 2-3 short-sleeve shirts (for hot afternoons)
- ☐ 2-3 pairs long trousers/zip-offs (versatile)
- ☐ 1-2 shorts (camp use)
- ☐ 1 warm fleece/pullover (cold mornings)
- ☐ 1 lightweight jacket/windbreaker
- ☐ 1 down vest or light puffer (May-Aug essential)
- ☐ Wide-brim hat (sun protection)
- ☐ Buff/bandana (dust protection)
- ☐ Warm beanie (winter mornings)

**Fabric Advice:**

- Quick-dry synthetics ideal
- Merino wool excellent (doesn't smell, multiple wears)
- Avoid cotton (slow to dry, heavy when wet)
- Moisture-wicking fabrics best

### Victoria Falls and town clothing

**Waterproof:**

- ☐ Rain jacket or poncho (for Falls spray)
- ☐ Waterproof bag/cover for camera
- ☐ Water-resistant shoes or sandals
- ☐ Quick-dry shorts and shirt

**Casual:**

- ☐ T-shirts and casual wear
- ☐ Comfortable walking shoes
- ☐ Sundress/casual dress (women)
- ☐ Collared shirt (men - some restaurants)
- ☐ Swimwear

### Clothing for activities

**White Water Rafting:**

- ☐ Swimwear
- ☐ Secure shoes (strapped sandals or old trainers)
- ☐ Rash guard/synthetic shirt (sun protection)
- ☐ Secure sunglasses (Croakies recommended)

**Hiking:**

- ☐ Broken-in hiking boots (not new!)
- ☐ Moisture-wicking socks (2-3 pairs)
- ☐ Hiking trousers/shorts
- ☐ Trekking poles (if multi-day)
- ☐ Gaiters (if rainy season)

**Upscale Lodges/Restaurants:**

- ☐ Smart casual outfit (1-2)
- ☐ Collared shirts
- ☐ Long trousers (not shorts)
- ☐ Closed-toe shoes
- ☐ Light dress or skirt (women)

## Footwear

**Essential:**

- ☐ Comfortable walking shoes/trainers (everyday)
- ☐ Sandals (camp, travel, Victoria Falls spray)
- ☐ Flip-flops (shower shoes, casual)

**Optional:**

- ☐ Hiking boots (if serious hiking planned)
- ☐ Water shoes (rafting, canoeing)
- ☐ Closed-toe safari shoes (many people prefer comfortable trainers)

**Tip:** Break in ANY new shoes months before your trip. Blisters ruin safaris.

## Health and medicines

**Prescription Medications:**

- ☐ All regular medications (bring extra supply)
- ☐ Prescription copies
- ☐ Antimalarial tablets (if your travel clinic recommends them)

**First Aid & OTC:**

- ☐ Pain relievers (ibuprofen, paracetamol)
- ☐ Antihistamine (allergies, insect bites)
- ☐ Anti-diarrhoea tablets
- ☐ Rehydration salts
- ☐ Motion sickness tablets
- ☐ Antacid tablets
- ☐ Blister plasters
- ☐ Plasters and antiseptic
- ☐ Antibiotic ointment

**Sun & Insect Protection:**

- ☐ High-factor sunscreen
- ☐ Lip balm with SPF
- ☐ After-sun lotion/aloe vera
- ☐ Insect repellent containing DEET (ask your travel clinic which strength)
- ☐ Anti-itch cream (hydrocortisone)

**Other:**

- ☐ Hand sanitiser
- ☐ Wet wipes
- ☐ Tissues/toilet paper (public restrooms often lack)
- ☐ Personal hygiene items
- ☐ Contact lens supplies (if applicable)

## Photography equipment

**Camera Gear:**

- ☐ Camera body
- ☐ Telephoto lens (70-300mm minimum, 100-400mm ideal for wildlife)
- ☐ Wide-angle lens (landscapes, Falls)
- ☐ Extra batteries (3-4 recommended)
- ☐ Memory cards (bring more than you think - wildlife shots add up!)
- ☐ Lens cleaning kit (dust everywhere)
- ☐ Camera bag (padded, dust-proof)
- ☐ Lens cloth
- ☐ Tripod or monopod (if serious photography)

**Protection:**

- ☐ Rain cover for camera
- ☐ Silica gel packs (moisture protection)
- ☐ Zip-lock bags (dust/rain backup)

**Mobile Photography:**

- ☐ Phone with good camera
- ☐ Portable power bank
- ☐ Phone mount/grip
- ☐ Protective case

**Binoculars:**

- ☐ 8x42 or 10x42 binoculars (essential for wildlife viewing)
- ☐ Neck strap
- ☐ Cleaning cloth

## Technology and electronics

**Essential:**

- ☐ Phone and charger
- ☐ Plug adapter (Zimbabwe uses UK-style three-pin plugs)
- ☐ Power bank (10,000+ mAh)
- ☐ Charging cables (bring extras!)
- ☐ Headphones

**Optional:**

- ☐ Laptop/tablet
- ☐ E-reader (great for downtime)
- ☐ Portable speaker (camp evenings)
- ☐ USB drive (backing up photos)
- ☐ Multi-port USB charger

**Important:**

- Power cuts are frequent, and many safari camps run on solar power or generators
- Some camps only have charging in the main area or at set times
- Bring a power bank
- Not all lodges have outlets in rooms (central charging stations)

## Toiletries and personal care

**Basics:**

- ☐ Toothbrush and toothpaste
- ☐ Shampoo and conditioner (travel size or buy locally)
- ☐ Soap/body wash (biodegradable preferred)
- ☐ Deodorant
- ☐ Razor
- ☐ Hairbrush/comb
- ☐ Nail clippers
- ☐ Tweezers

**Women:**

- ☐ Feminine hygiene products (bring supply - limited options in bush)
- ☐ Makeup (minimal recommended - heat and dust)
- ☐ Hair ties/headband

**Men:**

- ☐ Shaving supplies
- ☐ Beard care (if applicable)

**Eco-Friendly:**

- Use biodegradable products where possible

## Accessories and extras

**Useful:**

- ☐ Daypack/small backpack (game drives, day trips)
- ☐ Reusable water bottle (reduce plastic)
- ☐ Dry bag (water activities)
- ☐ Zip-lock bags (various sizes)
- ☐ Luggage locks (TSA-approved)
- ☐ Travel pillow
- ☐ Eye mask and earplugs
- ☐ Laundry detergent packets (hand washing)
- ☐ Clothesline/travel hangers
- ☐ Sewing kit

- ☐ Safety pins
- ☐ Notebook and pen
- ☐ Playing cards (downtime, flights)
- ☐ Travel towel (quick-dry microfiber)

**Safari Specific:**

- ☐ Head torch (camps are dark at night)
- ☐ Spare batteries
- ☐ Field guides (birds, mammals, trees)
- ☐ Safari journal

## Money and valuables

**Cash:**

- ☐ US dollars in small notes (US$1, US$5, US$10, US$20), because change is rarely available
- ☐ Clean, untorn notes. Shops often refuse torn or damaged notes, so don't accept them in change
- ☐ A money belt or pouch

Don't plan to leave with much cash: it's illegal to leave Zimbabwe with more than US$2,000 in cash, and you must declare US$10,000 or more when you arrive. See [currency and money](/essentials/currency-and-money).

**Cards:**

- ☐ Two cards (Visa or Mastercard), kept separately
- ☐ Notify bank of travel dates
- ☐ Check foreign transaction fees

**Security:**

- ☐ Money belt or neck pouch
- ☐ RFID-blocking wallet
- ☐ Photocopies of cards (separate from cards)

## What not to pack

**Leave at Home:**

- ❌ Excessive clothing (re-wear safari clothes - it's expected)
- ❌ Jewellery and valuables (unnecessary risk)
- ❌ Hair dryer (lodges provide or not needed)
- ❌ Full-size toiletries (buy locally or use lodge supplies)
- ❌ More than 2 pairs shoes (take up space)
- ❌ Hard-shell suitcase (won't fit on small planes)
- ❌ Books (heavy - use e-reader)
- ❌ Camouflage clothing (illegal for civilians)
- ❌ Drones, unless you have written permission from the Civil Aviation Authority of Zimbabwe before you arrive. Customs can confiscate them, and national parks need separate permission (see [travel advice](/essentials/travel-advice))

## What to buy locally

**Easy to buy in Zimbabwe:**

- Bottled water
- Snacks and food
- Basic toiletries
- Sunscreen (if you run out)
- Insect repellent
- Souvenirs and crafts
- Local SIM card
- Batteries (widely available)

**Where to Buy:**

- Supermarkets in Victoria Falls, Harare, Bulawayo
- Lodges sell essentials (marked up prices)
- Fuel station shops along main roads
- Pharmacies in towns

## Packing tips

**Rolling or folding:**

- Roll clothes (saves space, reduces wrinkles)
- Use packing cubes (organisation, compression)
- Stuff socks inside shoes (space efficiency)

**Weight Distribution:**

- Heavy items on bottom
- Frequently needed items on top
- Valuables in carry-on
- Distribute weight evenly

**Compression:**

- Packing cubes
- Compression bags (but consider wrinkles)
- Wear bulkiest items on flight (boots, jacket)

**Organisation:**

- Packing cubes by category (tops, bottoms, underwear)
- Zip-lock bags (toiletries, wet items, dirty clothes)
- Camera gear in padded bag
- Chargers/electronics in one place

**Last-Minute Check:**

- Lay everything out before packing
- Wear each outfit mentally through trip
- Remove non-essentials ruthlessly
- Weigh bag before leaving home

## Luxury or budget packing

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Luxury lodges">
    **Pack Less - Lodges Provide:**
    - Laundry service (daily in most luxury lodges)
    - Toiletries (high-end products)
    - Hairdryers
    - Bathrobes and slippers
    - Insect repellent
    - Sunscreen sometimes

    **You Only Need:**
    - 3-4 safari outfits (they'll wash daily)
    - 1 smart casual dinner outfit
    - Personal medications
    - Camera gear
    - Minimal toiletries

    **Total:** Can pack very light (8-10kg easily)

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Budget and camping">
    **Bring More - Fewer Services:**
    - More clothing (less frequent washing)
    - All toiletries
    - Towel (quick-dry travel towel)
    - Laundry detergent
    - More first aid supplies
    - Snacks and some food
    - Toilet paper
    - Camp chair (if camping)
    - Torch/headlamp essential

    **Total:** check this against any light-aircraft limit

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

**Work Essentials:**

## Pre-trip checklist

**2 Weeks Before:**

- ☐ Check passport validity
- ☐ Print travel documents
- ☐ Purchase travel insurance
- ☐ Start antimalarials when your clinic tells you to (some start days or weeks before travel)
- ☐ Break in new shoes/boots
- ☐ Test camera equipment
- ☐ Buy any missing gear
- ☐ Vaccinations done (ideally at least eight weeks before you go)

**1 Week Before:**

- ☐ Notify bank of travel
- ☐ Check weather forecast
- ☐ Adjust packing list seasonally
- ☐ Download offline maps
- ☐ Download entertainment
- ☐ Charge all electronics
- ☐ Fill prescriptions
- ☐ Get US dollars in small notes

**Day Before:**

- ☐ Weigh luggage
- ☐ Check flight status
- ☐ Print boarding passes
- ☐ Pack carry-on essentials
- ☐ Secure home
- ☐ Final document check

## Hand luggage essentials

**Never Check These:**

- Passport and documents
- Valuables and jewellery
- Medications (all)
- Camera equipment
- Phone and chargers
- Change of clothes (in case checked bag delayed)
- Liquids in containers of 100ml or less
- Important contacts
- Entertainment for flight

**Why:**

- Luggage delays common
- Theft risk
- Need medications immediately
- Camera gear too valuable to risk

## Final tips

**The 80/20 Rule:**
You'll wear 20% of what you pack 80% of the time. Pack accordingly.

**When in Doubt:**

- Choose versatile over specialized
- Prefer lightweight over heavy
- Pack layers rather than bulky items
- Bring items that serve multiple purposes
- Remember: you can wash clothes, buy essentials, and survive without most things

**Most Commonly Forgotten:**

- Phone charger
- Medications
- Sunglasses
- Hat
- Insect repellent
- Adapter plugs
- Binoculars
- Cash in small bills

**Most regretted packing:**

- Too many clothes
- Heavy books
- Unnecessary shoes
- Jewellery
- Hair styling tools
- Full-size toiletries
