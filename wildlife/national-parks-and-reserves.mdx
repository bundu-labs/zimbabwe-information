![Elephants in woodland](/images/wildlife/elephants-woodland.jpg)

**Zimbabwe's national parks are run by the Zimbabwe Parks and Wildlife Management Authority (ZimParks), which oversees about 5 million hectares, roughly an eighth of the country.** The big safari parks are Hwange, Mana Pools, Gonarezhou and Matusadona. Matobo is the best park for rhino on foot, and Zambezi National Park is minutes from Victoria Falls. International visitors pay US$12 to US$30 a day in conservation fees at most parks in 2026, and US$58 at the Victoria Falls Rainforest.

**Written by [Bryan Fawcett](/about-this-guide).**

## Park profiles

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Hwange">
    **Zimbabwe's largest national park**, in the north-west of the country.

    - **Size:** about 14,650 km², managed by ZimParks in three units: Main Camp, Sinamatella and Robins.
    - **Wildlife:** about 45,800 elephants (ZimParks' National Elephant Management Plan 2021–2025), about 100 mammal species and around 400 bird species. Lion, leopard, cheetah and painted dogs are all present.
    - **Water:** nearly 60 artificial water points supplement the natural pans, so game stays visible through the dry season.
    - **Access:** Main Camp is about 195 km, roughly 2.5 hours, from Victoria Falls.
    - **Stay:** ZimParks lodges, chalets and campsites at Main Camp, Sinamatella and Robins, and private lodges in concessions. A one-bedroom ZimParks chalet at Main Camp costs US$60 a night for international visitors in 2026.
    - **Best time:** the dry season, roughly May to October.

    See our [Hwange National Park guide](/destinations/hwange-national-park).

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Mana Pools">
    **A UNESCO World Heritage Site on the Zambezi River**, famous for walking and canoeing.

    - **Size:** 2,196 km². With the Sapi and Chewore Safari Areas it forms the World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1984 and covering 6,766 km².
    - **Wildlife:** elephant, lion, leopard, painted dogs, buffalo, hippo and crocodile, and about 380 bird species.
    - **Walking:** one of the few parks in Africa where you may walk without a guide, on the open floodplain only and with a permit. See [walking and canoe safaris](/planning/walking-and-canoe-safaris).
    - **Access:** about 390 km, around 7 hours, from Harare. Collect your permit at Marongora, then drive about 75 km of rough gravel down the escarpment. A 4x4 is recommended.
    - **Season:** the park is largely closed in the main rains, roughly mid-November or December to March. ZimParks sets the dates each year.
    - **Camping:** a Nyamepi riverfront site costs US$155 a night for international visitors in 2026. Popular sites fill months ahead.

    See our [Mana Pools guide](/destinations/mana-pools).

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Gonarezhou">
    **Zimbabwe's second-largest national park**, in the remote south-east, with a 100 km border with Mozambique.

    - **Size:** 5,053 km², from the Save River to the Mwenezi River.
    - **Managed by:** the Gonarezhou Conservation Trust (ZimParks and the Frankfurt Zoological Society) since 2017.
    - **Wildlife:** over 11,500 elephants and more than 450 bird species (the Trust), and all of the Big Five (ZimParks).
    - **Landmark:** the red sandstone Chilojo Cliffs along the Runde River.
    - **Access:** Chipinda Pools is about 55 km from Chiredzi. A high-clearance vehicle is the minimum and a 4x4 is recommended.
    - **Closed:** to all tourists from 10 January to 1 March, and remote camps close from late November.
    - **Stay:** Trust-run campsites from US$100 to US$180 per site for international visitors, and two bush camps at US$720 per person.

    See our [Gonarezhou guide](/destinations/gonarezhou).

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Matusadona">
    **A lakeshore park on the southern side of Lake Kariba**, between the Ume and Sanyati rivers.

    - **Size:** 1,470 km² (ZimParks).
    - **Managed by:** African Parks with ZimParks, under a 20-year agreement signed in November 2019.
    - **Wildlife:** elephant, buffalo, lion and leopard. A founder group of black rhino was flown in on 3 June 2026, to be released into a 175 km² Intensive Protection Zone.
    - **Access:** across the lake by boat or charter flight from Kariba. The only road in is a long gravel route from Karoi via the Binga road, ending 68 km from Tashinga.

    See our [Matusadona guide](/destinations/matusadona-national-park).

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Matobo">
    **Granite hills, rock art and rhino**, south of Bulawayo.

    - **Size:** about 424 km² (ZimParks). The wider Matobo Hills World Heritage Site, inscribed in 2003, covers 2,050 km².
    - **Wildlife:** black and white rhino, leopard, sable and many raptors. ZimParks says the park is one of three Intensive Rhino Protection Zones in the parks estate, and has the world's highest concentration of breeding black (Verreaux's) eagles.
    - **Access:** Matopos Road runs about 30 km south from Bulawayo to the park boundary on tar. Most park roads are gravel but suitable for most cars.

    See our [Matobo Hills guide](/destinations/matobo-hills).

  </Tab>

  <Tab title="Other parks">
    - **[Zambezi National Park](/destinations/zambezi-national-park):** upstream of Victoria Falls, with about 40 km of Zambezi River frontage. ZimParks gives 56,000 hectares for Zambezi and Victoria Falls national parks together.
    - **[Victoria Falls Rainforest](/destinations/victoria-falls):** Victoria Falls National Park covers about 23.4 km² and was inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 1989. Entry is US$58 for international visitors in 2026.
    - **[Chizarira](/destinations/chizarira-national-park):** 1,910 km² of gorges and miombo woodland on the Zambezi Escarpment. Very remote.
    - **[Nyanga](/destinations/nyanga):** in the Eastern Highlands. Mt Nyangani, at 2,592 m, is Zimbabwe's highest point, and Mutarazi Falls (762 m) in the south of the park is its highest waterfall (ZimParks).
    - **[Chimanimani](/destinations/chimanimani):** mountain hiking on the Mozambique border, with huts and campsites.
    - **[Lake Chivero](/destinations/lake-chivero)** and **[Chinhoyi Caves](/destinations/chinhoyi-caves):** easy trips from Harare.
    - **[Umfurudzi](/destinations/umfurudzi-park):** a 760 km² safari area about 130 km north-east of Harare, restocked with plains game, buffalo and elephant since 2011.

  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Fees, permits and booking

- **Conservation fees:** set by ZimParks and paid per person per day. They range from US$12 to US$30 for international visitors at most parks in 2026, and US$58 at the Victoria Falls Rainforest. See the full table on [park fees](/essentials/park-fees).
- **Vehicle fees:** a foreign-registered car pays US$10 a day at most parks (US$3 if staying overnight), and US$5 a day at Matusadona and Gonarezhou.
- **Children:** the 2026 tariff does not list a separate child rate. Ask at the gate.
- **Pay in US dollars,** in small notes. Card machines at remote gates do not always work. Keep your receipt.
- **Lodges** often include park fees in their rates. Ask before you pay twice.
- **Book ZimParks camps and lodges** through ZimParks: info@zimparks.org.zw, +263 242 707625, corner of Sandringham and Borrowdale Roads, Harare. Gonarezhou is booked through the Gonarezhou Conservation Trust.

## Who runs the parks

Most parks are run directly by ZimParks. Two big parks are co-managed with conservation partners:

- **Gonarezhou:** the Gonarezhou Conservation Trust, a ZimParks and Frankfurt Zoological Society partnership, fully operational since 1 March 2017.
- **Matusadona:** African Parks, under a 20-year agreement signed with ZimParks in 2019. It was the first Zimbabwean park to come under African Parks.

## Safari areas and communal land

Around the national parks are **safari areas**, where ZimParks allows regulated hunting and photographic tourism, such as the Sapi and Chewore Safari Areas next to Mana Pools.

On communal land, the **CAMPFIRE** programme (Communal Areas Management Programme for Indigenous Resources) lets communities manage wildlife through their Rural District Councils and earn income from it. The CAMPFIRE Association says the programme runs in 58 districts and protects about 50,000 km² of wildlife habitat, and that most of its income comes from levies on hunting and photographic safari operators.

## Transfrontier conservation areas

- **Kavango-Zambezi (KAZA):** about 520,000 km² across Angola, Botswana, Namibia, Zambia and Zimbabwe, according to the KAZA secretariat. Hwange and the Victoria Falls area are in the Zimbabwean part. The [KAZA UniVisa](/essentials/visas-and-entry) lets you move between Zimbabwe and Zambia.
- **Great Limpopo Transfrontier Park:** set up by a treaty signed in December 2002 by Mozambique, South Africa and Zimbabwe. SANParks describes it as about 35,000 km², linking Gonarezhou with Kruger and Limpopo national parks.

## Planning tips

- **Go in the dry season** (roughly May to October) for the best game viewing. October is very hot. See [best time for a safari](/wildlife/best-time-for-safari).
- **Check road conditions and closures.** Mana Pools and Gonarezhou close or partly close in the rains.
- **Bring everything** to remote parks: food, water, fuel and spares. There are no shops inside the parks.
- **Read the rules.** Stay on roads, keep clear of elephants with calves and lions with cubs, and take your rubbish out.
