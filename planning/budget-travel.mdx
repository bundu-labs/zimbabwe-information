**The cheapest way to see Zimbabwe's wildlife is to self-drive and stay in ZimParks campsites and chalets.** In 2026 a campsite at Hwange costs US$23 a night for up to three people, and a one-bedroom chalet at Main Camp costs US$60, on top of a US$24 daily park fee for international visitors. The big fixed costs are the visa (US$30 single entry for most nationalities), park fees and transport, because distances are long. Zimbabwe uses US dollars, so bring cash in small notes.

## Where to sleep cheaply

### ZimParks campsites and chalets

ZimParks runs campsites, chalets and lodges in most national parks. They are the best value for self-drivers. 2026 prices for international visitors:

| Where                                              | 2026 price (US$)        |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Hwange camping or caravan site (up to 3 people)    | 23 per site per night   |
| Hwange bush camps                                  | 35 per person per night |
| Hwange Main Camp chalet, one bedroom               | 60 per night            |
| Matobo (Maleme) camping                            | 12 per person           |
| Chimanimani mountain hut or campsite               | 12 per person           |
| Lake Chivero camping                               | 12 per person           |
| Nyanyana, Kariba, camping                          | 12 per person           |
| Zambezi National Park camping                      | 35 per person per night |
| Mana Pools, Nyamepi standard site (up to 6 people) | 120 per site            |

Book through ZimParks at info@zimparks.org.zw or +263 242 707625. The prices above are for accommodation only: the park fee is extra.

### Gonarezhou

The Gonarezhou Conservation Trust runs its own campsites. A developed campsite such as Chipinda Pools has a minimum charge of US$104 a night for international visitors (up to six adults), so it's cheap if you share. Book at reservations@gonarezhou.org. See [Gonarezhou](/destinations/gonarezhou).

### Towns

Victoria Falls, Harare and Bulawayo all have backpacker hostels, campsites and guesthouses. Prices and quality change often, so read recent reviews and book direct. See our [accommodation guide](/essentials/accommodation).

## Saving money on safari

- **Self-drive and camp.** Splitting a car and a campsite between three people is far cheaper than a lodge. A 4x4 is needed for the remote parks. At Matobo, ZimParks says most park roads are gravel but suitable for most vehicles.
- **Do a day visit.** Zambezi National Park is about 6 km from Victoria Falls town, and Matobo is about 30 km from Bulawayo, so you can visit without staying overnight.
- **Stay overnight where it's cheaper.** At Hwange, the overnight rate (US$12) is half the day rate (US$24).
- **Share boats and guided activities** with other travellers.
- **Travel in the rains** (roughly November to April) for lower lodge rates. But check closures: Mana Pools is largely closed in the main rains, and Gonarezhou closes completely from 10 January to 1 March.

## Getting around

- **Distances are long.** Harare to Victoria Falls is about 878 km and Harare to Kariba about 365 km. See [travel distances](/essentials/distances) and [transportation](/essentials/transportation).
- **Intercity buses and coaches** link the main towns. Choose established companies, travel by day, and keep your bags close.
- **Kombis** (minibuses) are the cheapest local transport, but they leave when full.
- **The Kariba–Mlibizi ferry** crosses Lake Kariba in about 22 hours. In 2026 an adult fare is US$150 one way, with meals, and a car up to 4 m costs US$140. It replaces a drive of more than 1,250 km around the lake. See [Lake Kariba](/destinations/lake-kariba).
- **Car hire** is often the most practical option for a group. Check the insurance, the mileage limit and whether the car may enter national parks.

## Money tips

- **Bring US dollars in small, clean notes.** The US dollar is legal tender and used for most things. You may get change in the local currency, the ZiG.
- **Change money only at banks or licensed bureaux de change.**
- **Card machines at remote park gates don't always work,** so carry cash for park fees.
- **Don't leave Zimbabwe with more than US$2,000 in cash.**

See [currency and money](/essentials/currency-and-money).

## Staying connected

Buy a local SIM from Econet or NetOne for data and calls. You need your passport to register. See [communication](/essentials/communication) for current bundle prices.

## Don't cut these corners

- **Travel insurance** with medical evacuation. Many parks are hours from a hospital. See [travel insurance](/essentials/travel-insurance).
- **Malaria prevention** in the Zambezi Valley, Hwange and the lowveld. See [health and safety](/essentials/health-and-safety).
- **Licensed guides** for walking. See [walking and canoe safaris](/planning/walking-and-canoe-safaris).
- **Don't drive at night.** Roads are unlit and animals and pedestrians are hard to see.
