**Most of Zimbabwe's national parks have campsites run by the Zimbabwe Parks and Wildlife Management Authority (ZimParks).** They range from campsites with ablution blocks at Hwange Main Camp and Mana Pools' Nyamepi, to exclusive riverside sites with only a long-drop toilet and a braai area. You book through ZimParks, bring all your own gear and food, and pay camping fees on top of the daily park conservation fees. Gonarezhou is the main exception: its campsites are run and booked by the Gonarezhou Conservation Trust.

**Written by [Bryan Fawcett](/about-this-guide).**

<Note>
  **Last reviewed: 25 October 2026** against the official ZimParks 2026 tariff (["De-Gazetted Tariff 2026"](https://www.zimparks.org.zw/media/2026/02/De-Gazetted-Tariff-2026.pdf)), the [ZimParks booking FAQs](https://book.zimparks.org.zw/booking_faqs), ZimParks park pages and the Gonarezhou Conservation Trust's camping page. Campsites open, close and change. Confirm with ZimParks before you travel.

ZimParks also has campsites at recreational parks and other sites, including [Lake Chivero](/destinations/lake-chivero), Lake Mutirikwi (Kyle), Ngezi, Sebakwe, [Chinhoyi Caves](/destinations/chinhoyi-caves) and Nyanyana near Kariba. The 2026 tariff doesn't list camping in [Matusadona](/destinations/matusadona-national-park), which is managed by African Parks, so ask ZimParks what is open before you plan a self-drive trip there.

## What the different sites provide

ZimParks describes its camping types like this:

- **Campsites:** a camping ground and ablutions. You bring everything else: tent, bedding, lighting, cooking gear, food and drinking water.
- **Overnight picnic sites (Hwange):** inside the game area, with a kitchen and bathroom. You bring camping equipment. Each site takes up to 6 people.
- **Bush camps:** inside the game area, with no ablutions.
- **Exclusive camping sites (Mana Pools):** upstream along the Zambezi, with only a braai area and a long-drop toilet.

Most ZimParks camps are **not fenced**, so wildlife can walk through. Developed campsites in Gonarezhou have shared ablutions and an attendant but no electricity; its bush, wilderness and transit sites have only a bush loo and a fire pit, and no drinking water.

## Camping fees in 2026

ZimParks camping fees are charged on top of the [daily conservation fees](/essentials/park-fees). Some are per person and some are per site, so check which applies. The main 2026 international rates are:

- **Hwange:** US$23 per campsite (up to 3 people), US$35 per person in a bush camp, and US$235 per overnight picnic site (up to 6 people)
- **Mana Pools:** US$120 for a Nyamepi standard site, US$155 for a Nyamepi riverfront site, US$235 for an exclusive camp and US$355 for Chitake 1 or 3B (each up to 6 people)
- **Matobo, Chimanimani and Lake Chivero:** US$12 per person
- **Zambezi National Park:** US$35 per person
- **Chizarira:** US$40 per person per night (exclusive camping)

See the full table on our [park fees page](/essentials/park-fees#camping-fees-us), including local rates. Gonarezhou sets its own camping rates, from US$100 to US$180 per site a night for international visitors in 2026, with minimum numbers per site. See our [Gonarezhou guide](/destinations/gonarezhou).

## How to book

<Steps>
  <Step title="Book with ZimParks">
    Book online through the [ZimParks booking site](https://book.zimparks.org.zw), which covers camping, lodges, chalets and picnic sites, or email ZimParks Reservations at bookings@zimparks.org.zw. The head office is at the Botanical Gardens, on the corner of Sandringham and Borrowdale roads in Harare. Gonarezhou campsites are booked with the [Gonarezhou Conservation Trust](https://gonarezhou.org/visit-gonarezhou-national-park/camping-gonarezhou-national-park/).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pay">
    Online, ZimParks accepts Visa and Mastercard cards and EcoCash. At the parks, it accepts cash in US dollars or ZiG. Bring small US dollar notes, because card machines at remote gates don't always work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Know the cancellation rules">
    According to ZimParks, cancelling more than 14 days before check-in gets a 50% refund, and later cancellations get no refund. The first change to a booking is free; later changes cost a 15% administration fee.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Book early for the dry season">
    Mana Pools' Nyamepi riverfront sites, the exclusive camps and Chitake fill months ahead for July to October. Mana Pools is largely closed in the main rains, roughly mid-November or December to March.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Mana Pools permits:** ZimParks says every visitor must report at the **Marongora** office on the Harare–Chirundu road for a Zambezi Valley entry permit, or at the **Chirundu** office if you come in from Chirundu. You need a high-clearance vehicle for the roads into the park.
</Warning>

## What to bring

- **All your camping equipment:** tent, bedding, chairs, table and lighting. Gear is rarely available for hire in the parks.
- **All your food and drinking water.** There are no shops inside most parks. In Mana Pools, ZimParks says fruit isn't allowed, and you'll want extra ice in hot months.
- **Extra lighting**, such as lanterns or rechargeable torches.
- **Insect repellent and malaria tablets** where advised. See [health and safety](/essentials/health-and-safety).
- **Warm clothes.** Nights from May to August can be cold, close to freezing in the highlands.
- **Recovery gear** if you're self-driving: spare tyres, a jack, a tow rope and a shovel.
- **Sun protection** and a first aid kit.

## Staying safe in an unfenced camp

- **Keep food locked away** in your vehicle. Baboons, monkeys and hyenas raid camps.
- **Use a torch after dark** and look around before you walk to the ablutions. Elephants, buffalo, hippos and lions can come into camps.
- **Don't walk outside camp** without a licensed guide, except where park rules allow it. In Mana Pools, unguided walking needs a permit and is allowed only on the open floodplain. See [walking and canoe safaris](/planning/walking-and-canoe-safaris).
- **In Chimanimani, don't camp near rivers in the rainy season.** ZimParks warns that flash floods are common and dangerous.
- **Use fire only in braai areas**, and put fires out completely.
