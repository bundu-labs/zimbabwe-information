**Zimbabwe is one of Africa's most rewarding and least crowded safari countries.** It has Victoria Falls, five UNESCO World Heritage Sites, some of the continent's best-trained guides, and walking and canoe safaris you won't find elsewhere. This guide covers everything you need to plan a trip: entry rules, safety, money, seasons, where to go, what it costs and how to get around.

It is written and kept current by people who live and work in Zimbabwe, not by visitors passing through.

**Written by [Bryan Fawcett](/about-this-guide)**, with local contributors.

## Is Zimbabwe safe to visit?

**Yes, for most travellers.** Zimbabwe's tourism areas — Victoria Falls, Hwange, Kariba, Mana Pools, Matobo and Great Zimbabwe — see millions of safe visits, and Zimbabweans are known across the region for their warmth towards guests. Most problems visitors face are the same as elsewhere in southern Africa: petty theft in city centres, poor roads at night and wildlife risk in unfenced parks.

The US, UK and Australian governments all keep travel advisories for Zimbabwe. None of them advise against travel to the main tourist areas. We summarise what each one says, and what it means on the ground, in our dedicated page.

## Entry requirements and visas

- **Passport:** valid for at least six months after you arrive, with at least three blank pages (six if you are travelling through South Africa).
- **Visa:** citizens of most SADC countries need no visa. Many other nationalities, including UK, US, EU, Canadian and Australian citizens, can get a visa on arrival or apply for an e-visa before travelling. A few nationalities must apply in advance.
- **KAZA UniVisa:** US$50 for 30 days, covering Zimbabwe and Zambia plus day trips into Botswana. It is sold at Harare, Bulawayo and Victoria Falls airports and at the Victoria Falls and Kazungula borders. Availability can change at short notice.
- **Yellow fever:** you need a certificate only if you are aged 9 months or over and arrive from a country with yellow fever risk, including airport transits of more than 12 hours there.
- **Pay in cash:** carry clean, recent US dollar notes for visa fees at the border.

Fees, eligible nationalities and the UniVisa's availability are listed on our [visas and entry page](/essentials/visas-and-entry). Always confirm on the official [Zimbabwe e-visa portal](https://www.evisa.gov.zw) before you fly.

## Money: what currency to bring

**Bring US dollars in cash, in small notes ($1, $5, $10, $20).** The US dollar is used for almost every tourism transaction: lodges, activities, park fees, visas and tips. Change is often hard to find, and torn, marked or old (pre-2006) notes are often refused. You can take no more than US$2,000 in cash out of the country unless you declare it.

- **Cards:** Visa is widely accepted in hotels, lodges and larger shops in Harare, Bulawayo and Victoria Falls. Mastercard is less reliable. Many smaller businesses are cash only.
- **ATMs:** some dispense US dollars, but supply is unreliable. Don't depend on them.
- **ZiG (ZWG):** the local currency since April 2024. You may receive it as change in supermarkets. You don't need to buy it in advance.
- **Mobile money:** EcoCash is used everywhere by locals, but needs a Zimbabwean number and account.

Read the full [currency and money guide](/essentials/currency-and-money) and our [tipping guide](/essentials/tipping-guide).

## When to visit Zimbabwe

| Season              | Months              | Good for                                                                  | Keep in mind                                                                               |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Cool dry season** | May – August        | Wildlife viewing, walking safaris, comfortable days                       | Cold early mornings and nights (near freezing in the highlands). Peak prices July – August |
| **Hot dry season**  | September – October | The best game viewing of the year, as animals gather at water             | Very hot, up to 40°C in the Zambezi Valley. Victoria Falls is at its lowest                |
| **Early rains**     | November – December | Green season prices, migrant birds arrive, newborn antelope               | Afternoon storms. Some remote camps close                                                  |
| **Wet season**      | January – April     | Victoria Falls at full flow (peak around April), lush landscapes, birding | Some roads impassable. Mana Pools closes to vehicles. Higher malaria risk                  |

See [when to visit](/when-to-visit) and [weather by region](/essentials/weather-by-region) for month-by-month detail.

## Things to do in Zimbabwe

- **Go on safari** — game drives, [walking safaris](/planning/safari-planning) with some of Africa's most rigorously licensed guides, canoe trips on the Zambezi and night drives in private concessions.
- **See Victoria Falls** from the rainforest walk, by helicopter, from Devil's Pool on the Zambian side, or from below on a [white-water rafting](/adventure/water-sports) trip.
- **Explore 900 years of history** at [Great Zimbabwe](/destinations/great-zimbabwe), [Khami](/heritage/khami-ruins) and [Naletale](/heritage/naletale-ruins), and see [San rock art](/rock-art) up to 13,000 years old.
- **Hike** Mount Nyangani, the [Chimanimani mountains](/destinations/chimanimani) and [World's View](/scenic/worlds-view-nyanga). See our [hiking guide](/adventure/hiking-and-trekking).
- **Meet the culture** — Shona sculpture, mbira music, local food like sadza and nyama, and the [festivals](/culture/festivals-and-events) that fill the calendar.

See all [activities and experiences](/adventure/activities-and-experiences) and [things to do by region](/adventure/things-to-do-by-region).

## How much does a trip to Zimbabwe cost?

Prices below are per person per day in US dollars, and include accommodation, food and local transport but not international flights.

| Travel style     | Per day     | What you get                                                                      |
| ---------------- | ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Budget**       | $50 – $100  | Backpackers and campsites, local food, shared transport, self-guided visits       |
| **Mid-range**    | $150 – $350 | Comfortable lodges and guesthouses, some guided activities, car hire or transfers |
| **Safari lodge** | $400 – $900 | Full-board camps with game drives and walks included                              |
| **Luxury**       | $1,000+     | Top private-concession camps, light-aircraft transfers, private guiding           |

National park conservation fees (US$12–58 per person for an international day visit in 2026, see [park fees](/essentials/park-fees)), activities such as rafting and helicopter flights, and tips are usually extra. Read our [budget travel](/planning/budget-travel) and [luxury travel](/planning/luxury-travel) guides.

## Getting to and around Zimbabwe

- **By air:** most visitors fly into Victoria Falls (VFA) or Harare (HRE), usually through Johannesburg, Nairobi, Addis Ababa or Dubai. See the [airport guide](/essentials/airport-guide).
- **Overland:** main borders are [Beitbridge](/essentials/beitbridge-border) (South Africa), Kazungula (Botswana), Victoria Falls Bridge (Zambia), Chirundu (Zambia) and Forbes (Mozambique).
- **Getting around:** domestic flights and charters link the parks. Self-drive is possible on main roads, but avoid driving at night. Intercity coaches link Harare, Bulawayo, Victoria Falls and Mutare.

See [transportation](/essentials/transportation) and [distances between destinations](/essentials/distances).

## Zimbabwe travel FAQs

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need a visa for Zimbabwe?">
    Most visitors do. Citizens of most SADC countries are visa-exempt. UK, US, EU, Canadian and Australian citizens can get a visa on arrival or an e-visa before travelling. Check your nationality on our [visa guide](/essentials/visas-and-entry).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is Zimbabwe safe for tourists?">
    Yes, for most travellers. The main tourist areas are safe and welcoming. Take normal precautions against petty theft in cities, don't drive at night and follow your guide's instructions in the parks. Read our full [travel advice page](/essentials/travel-advice).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What currency should I take to Zimbabwe?">
    US dollars in cash, in small, clean notes. Cards work in hotels and larger businesses, but many smaller places are cash only. See the [money guide](/essentials/currency-and-money).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What is the best time to visit Zimbabwe?">
    May to October for wildlife, with September and October the peak. Victoria Falls is at its fullest from February to May. See [when to visit](/when-to-visit).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do I need malaria tablets for Zimbabwe?">
    Most likely, yes. Official advice treats malaria as a risk everywhere except Harare and Bulawayo. Risk is highest in the low-lying safari areas: Victoria Falls, Hwange, Kariba, Mana Pools and Gonarezhou. Ask a travel clinic about antimalarials. See [health and safety](/essentials/health-and-safety).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How many days do I need in Zimbabwe?">
    Seven days covers Victoria Falls and Hwange. Ten to fourteen days lets you add Mana Pools, Matobo, Great Zimbabwe or the Eastern Highlands. See our [itineraries](/planning/quick-itineraries).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I visit both sides of Victoria Falls?">
    Yes. The KAZA UniVisa, when available, covers Zimbabwe and Zambia. Otherwise buy a Zambian day visa at the bridge. The Zimbabwean side has more of the falls in view.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Is Zimbabwe expensive?">
    Safari lodges are priced in US dollars and are comparable to Botswana or Zambia at the top end. Budget travel is possible from around $50 a day using backpackers, campsites and local transport.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
