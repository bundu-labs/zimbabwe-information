Harare, Zimbabwe's capital and largest city, offers visitors a blend of urban energy, cultural attractions, and leafy tranquillity. Often used as a gateway to Zimbabwe's natural wonders, the city deserves exploration in its own right, with its national gallery and museums, busy markets and jacaranda-lined avenues.

**Written by [Bryan Fawcett](/about-this-guide).**

## Why visit Harare

While many travellers pass straight through on the way to Zimbabwe's safari areas, Harare rewards those who linger with:

- **Contemporary urban life** in Zimbabwe's capital
- **Museums and galleries** covering the country's history and art
- **Green spaces**, from Harare Gardens to the Mukuvisi Woodlands
- **Busy markets** for crafts and everyday shopping
- **A lively arts scene**, with galleries and theatre
- **Easy day trips** to rock art sites, lakes and caves

## Top attractions

### Museums and cultural sites

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="National Gallery of Zimbabwe" icon="palette">
    Zimbabwe's national art institution, with changing exhibitions of Zimbabwean and African art, including stone sculpture.

    **Details:**
    - Location: 20 Julius Nyerere Way, next to Harare Gardens
    - Hours: daily 9:00 to 16:30, closed on public holidays
    - Adult admission: US$15 for international (non-African) visitors, US$5 for African residents and US$2 for Zimbabwean residents

    Source: National Gallery of Zimbabwe. The gallery also has branches in Bulawayo and Mutare.

  </Card>

  <Card title="National Archives of Zimbabwe" icon="book">
    Zimbabwe's national collection of historical documents and photographs, used by researchers and open to visitors.

    **Details:**
    - Location: Borrowdale Road, Gunhill
    - The Archives' website was not working when we checked, so confirm opening times locally before you go

  </Card>

  <Card title="National Heroes Acre" icon="monument">
    The national monument and burial ground for those declared national heroes, on a hill on the western side of the city, off the road to Bulawayo.

    **Details:**
    - Respectful behaviour and dress expected
    - Check visiting arrangements with National Museums and Monuments of Zimbabwe (NMMZ) before you go

  </Card>

  <Card title="Museum of Human Sciences" icon="people-group">
    NMMZ's regional museum for northern Zimbabwe, on Rotten Row, with archaeology and ethnography collections.

    **Details:**
    - NMMZ lists its museums as open daily, 8:00 to 16:30
    - NMMZ entrance fees range up to US$15, so check the price at the door

  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Parks and natural spaces

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Harare Gardens" icon="tree">
    The city-centre public park beside the National Gallery, with lawns, mature trees and walking paths. It is popular with locals at weekends.
  </Card>

  <Card title="National Botanic Garden" icon="seedling">
    Large gardens in Alexandra Park, planted with trees and shrubs from across Zimbabwe's vegetation zones. Good for a quiet walk and birdwatching. Check opening times and fees at the gate.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Mukuvisi Woodlands" icon="leaf">
    A woodland reserve in Harare's eastern suburbs, a short drive from the city centre.

    **Details:**
    - Open daily, 8:00 to 17:00
    - Entry: US$6 adults, US$5 children aged 5 to 12, under-5s free, US$3 senior citizens
    - Activities: walking, horse riding and bike riding, plus a coffee shop and gift shop
    - You must sign an indemnity before you enter

    Source: Mukuvisi Woodlands.

  </Card>

  <Card title="Lake Chivero Recreational Park" icon="water">
    A ZimParks recreational park about 32 km south-west of the city, around the reservoir that supplies Harare. Game viewing, birdwatching and fishing.

    **2026 ZimParks conservation fees (per person per day):** US$12 international, US$10 SADC, US$3 Zimbabwean. Vehicle fees are extra. See [park fees](/essentials/park-fees) and our [Lake Chivero guide](/destinations/lake-chivero).

  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Markets and shopping

<Card>
  **Mbare Musika:** one of Zimbabwe's largest and best-known markets, in the high-density suburb of Mbare. Fresh produce, household goods and crafts. It is busy and crowded, so go in the morning with a local guide and keep valuables out of sight.

**Craft and flea markets:** Avondale and other suburban shopping centres host craft and flea markets. Prices are usually negotiable.

**Shopping centres:** The northern suburbs, including Borrowdale and Avondale, have modern shopping centres with supermarkets, cafés and restaurants.

</Card>

## Neighbourhoods to explore

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion icon="building" title="Central business district (CBD)">
    The historic heart of Harare, with Africa Unity Square, Parliament and Harare Gardens nearby.

    - Best explored during business hours
    - Quieter at weekends, when many businesses close
    - Take normal urban precautions, especially after dark

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="utensils" title="Avondale and the Avenues">
    Close to the centre, with shopping centres, cafés and restaurants, and many guesthouses. Comfortable for walking in daylight.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="tree" title="Borrowdale and the northern suburbs">
    Leafy residential suburbs with shopping centres, restaurants, embassies and the racecourse. Spread out, so you will need a car or taxi.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="basket-shopping" title="Mbare">
    One of Harare's oldest high-density suburbs, home to Mbare Musika market and Rufaro Stadium. Visit with a local guide and be discreet with cameras.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Food and dining

Harare has the most varied dining in Zimbabwe, from local eateries to international restaurants, mostly in the northern suburbs and around shopping centres. Restaurants open and close often, so check recent reviews before you go.

**Dishes to try:**

- Sadza ne nyama (maize meal with meat stew)
- Muriwo une dovi (greens with peanut butter)
- Derere (okra)
- Madora (mopane worms), for the adventurous

**Theatre:** [Reps Theatre](https://www.reps.co.zw) stages productions through the year.

## Accommodation

<Card>
  Harare has hotels, lodges and guesthouses across all budgets. Hotels we could confirm on their operators' own websites:

- **The Rainbow Towers Hotel & Conference Centre** and the **New Ambassador Hotel**, both run by Rainbow Tourism Group (RTG)
- **Holiday Inn Harare**, run by African Sun

The suburbs, especially Avondale, Belgravia, Highlands and Borrowdale, have many guesthouses and lodges. Compare current rates and reviews on booking sites.

**Location advice:**

- Northern suburbs: quieter, but you will need transport
- City centre: convenient by day, less pleasant at night
- Avondale and the Avenues: a good compromise

</Card>

## Practical information

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion icon="clock" title="How much time to spend">
    - **Minimum:** one full day for the main sights
    - **Comfortable:** two to three days
    - **Extended:** four to five days, using Harare as a base for day trips

    **Suggested plan:**
    - Day 1: city centre, Harare Gardens and the National Gallery
    - Day 2: markets and Mukuvisi Woodlands
    - Day 3: day trip to Domboshawa or Lake Chivero

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="route" title="Getting around">
    - **Taxis:** the easiest option for visitors. Agree the fare before you set off, or ask your hotel to arrange one
    - **Ride-hailing apps:** check which apps work locally when you arrive
    - **Car hire:** available at the airport and in the city. Zimbabwe drives on the left
    - **Kombis (minibuses):** cheap but hard to use without local knowledge

    **Airport:** Robert Gabriel Mugabe International Airport is about 15 km south-east of the city centre. See our [airport guide](/essentials/airport-guide).

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="money-bill" title="Money">
    US dollars are widely used, and the local currency, Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG), also circulates. Bring small US dollar notes, and don't rely on ATMs accepting foreign cards. See our [currency and money guide](/essentials/currency-and-money).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="shield" title="Safety and etiquette">
    **Safety tips:**
    - Take normal urban precautions and keep valuables out of sight
    - Avoid walking at night, especially in the CBD
    - Don't photograph government buildings, police or military sites

    **Cultural notes:**
    - Greet people before getting down to business
    - Dress conservatively in official and business settings

    See our [travel advice and safety guide](/essentials/travel-advice).

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Day trips from Harare

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Domboshawa" icon="mountain">
    A granite dome about 30 km north of Harare, with rock paintings in a cave shelter near the top and wide views over the countryside.

    - Allow half a day
    - Local guides are available at the site
    - Check the entrance fee on arrival
    - Combine with nearby Ngomakurira for a full day

    See our [Domboshawa guide](/destinations/domboshava).

  </Card>

  <Card title="Great Zimbabwe" icon="monument">
    The UNESCO World Heritage site of stone ruins near Masvingo, best as an overnight trip.

    - About 320 km from Harare, around 4 hours' drive each way
    - Entry fee set by National Museums and Monuments of Zimbabwe (NMMZ). NMMZ lists site fees of up to US$15 but not the current Great Zimbabwe rate, so check at the gate
    - A guide adds a lot to the visit

    See our [Great Zimbabwe guide](/destinations/great-zimbabwe).

  </Card>

  <Card title="Chinhoyi Caves" icon="water">
    Limestone caves with a deep blue pool, about 125 km north-west of Harare on the road to Kariba.

    - Full-day trip from Harare
    - **2026 ZimParks conservation fee:** US$12 international, US$10 SADC, US$5 Zimbabwean per day

    See our [Chinhoyi Caves guide](/destinations/chinhoyi-caves).

  </Card>

  <Card title="Bushman Rock" icon="wine-glass">
    A vineyard estate and private game reserve about 40 minutes' drive from Harare, according to the estate.

    - Day trips with wine tasting, game viewing and horse riding
    - Book in advance: the estate says it does not accept walk-ins

  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Best time to visit

<Card>
  Harare sits at about 1,500 m, so it is rarely very hot. Average figures from the World Meteorological Organization (WMO):

**April to May**

- Average highs of 24 to 26°C, lows of 9 to 12°C
- The rains have usually ended and the countryside is still green

**June to August (winter)**

- Average highs of about 22 to 24°C, lows of 6 to 8°C
- Almost no rain and clear skies. Bring layers for cold mornings and evenings

**September to October**

- The hottest months, with average highs of 28 to 29°C
- Jacaranda trees flower across the city in September and October

**November to March (rainy season)**

- Average highs of about 26°C, with most of the year's rain falling in afternoon storms
- Green landscapes, but rain can disrupt outdoor plans

See [weather by month](/essentials/weather-by-month) for the full table.

</Card>

## Harare for different travellers

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Business travellers" icon="briefcase">
    - Most corporate offices are in the CBD and the northern suburbs
    - Business etiquette is more formal than in many Western countries
    - Power cuts happen, so carry a power bank and ask whether your hotel has a generator

  </Card>

  <Card title="Families" icon="children">
    - Mukuvisi Woodlands for walks and horse riding
    - Harare Gardens and the National Botanic Garden for space to run around
    - [Wild is Life](https://wildislife.org), a wildlife sanctuary in Harare. Bookings are essential
    - Lake Chivero for a day out with game viewing

  </Card>

  <Card title="Budget travellers" icon="money-bill">
    - Guesthouses in Avondale and the Avenues
    - Local eateries and markets for cheap meals
    - Harare Gardens is a public park
    - Hire a local guide for Mbare Musika

  </Card>

  <Card title="Culture seekers" icon="palette">
    - National Gallery of Zimbabwe
    - Reps Theatre
    - Mbare Musika with a local guide
    - Domboshawa's rock paintings

  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  **Harare insider tip:** Visit Mbare Musika in the morning with a local guide, when it is busiest. Then head back to the centre for a quieter afternoon at the National Gallery and Harare Gardens next door.
</Tip>
