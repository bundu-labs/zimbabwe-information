Bulawayo, Zimbabwe's second-largest city, offers visitors a distinctive blend of colonial history, architectural heritage, and cultural experiences. Set in the heart of Matabeleland, it is the gateway to the Matobo Hills and Khami, two of Zimbabwe's five UNESCO World Heritage sites, and moves at a more relaxed pace than the capital.

## Why visit Bulawayo

Often overlooked in favour of Victoria Falls or Harare, Bulawayo rewards visitors with:

- **Wide, tree-lined streets**, traditionally said to have been laid out wide enough to turn a full span of oxen
- **Colonial-era architecture** in the city centre
- **Ndebele heritage**, as the successor to King Lobengula's royal town
- **Museums**, including the Natural History Museum and the Railway Museum
- **A lively arts scene**, from Mzilikazi's art centre to the Intwasa festival
- **Easy access** to Matobo Hills and Khami
- **A friendlier pace** than the capital

## Top attractions

### Museums and historical sites

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Natural History Museum" icon="dinosaur">
    NMMZ's museum for western Zimbabwe, in Centenary Park. It holds large natural history collections, with mammal, bird and geology galleries, plus archaeology and history displays.

    **Details:**
    - NMMZ lists its museums as open daily, 8:00 to 16:30
    - NMMZ entrance fees range up to US$15, so check the price at the door
    - Allow two to three hours

  </Card>

  <Card title="Railway Museum" icon="train">
    Owned by National Railways of Zimbabwe (NRZ), behind Bulawayo station in the suburb of Raylton. The collection of steam locomotives and rolling stock includes Cecil Rhodes' private railway coach.

    **Details:**
    - We could not confirm current opening times or fees, so check locally before you go
    - Allow one to two hours

  </Card>

  <Card title="City centre and City Hall" icon="building-columns">
    The grid of wide streets around City Hall holds much of Bulawayo's colonial-era architecture. A statue of Joshua Nkomo, the nationalist leader often called "Father Zimbabwe", stands at the junction of Main Street and 8th Avenue.

    - Best explored on foot during the day
    - Many older buildings are offices, so view them from outside

  </Card>

  <Card title="Bulawayo Club" icon="martini-glass">
    A historic building that is still a working gentleman's club, with 15 guest rooms and a restaurant, according to the club.

    - Location: corner of 8th Avenue and Fort Street
    - Ask the club whether non-members can visit or dine

  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Cultural attractions

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="National Gallery in Bulawayo" icon="palette">
    The Bulawayo branch of the National Gallery of Zimbabwe, showing contemporary Zimbabwean art. Contact the National Gallery for current exhibitions, hours and admission.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Amakhosi Cultural Centre" icon="masks-theater">
    A long-established performing arts centre for theatre, dance and music. Its website was not live when we checked, so ask locally about the performance schedule.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Mzilikazi Art and Craft Centre" icon="paintbrush">
    A long-running art and craft school in Mzilikazi township, named after King Mzilikazi. Sculptor Adam Madebe trained and later taught ceramics here. Ask locally about visiting the studios and buying students' work.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Old Bulawayo" icon="monument">
    A heritage site south of the city with a reconstruction of King Lobengula's royal town. Check with National Museums and Monuments of Zimbabwe (NMMZ) that it is open before you make the trip.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Parks and recreation

<Card>
  **Centenary Park and Central Park**

- Public parks in the city centre, next to the Natural History Museum
- Good for a walk or picnic

**Hillside Dams**

- Two small dams in the suburbs, with walking paths and birdwatching
- Check the entry fee at the gate

**Tshabalala Sanctuary**

- A small ZimParks sanctuary about 10 km south of the city, on the road to Matobo
- Plains game such as giraffe, zebra and antelope, and good birding
- **2026 ZimParks conservation fee:** US$12 international, US$10 SADC, US$3 Zimbabwean per day

</Card>

## Neighbourhoods to explore

  <Accordion title="City centre" icon="building">
    The historic heart of Bulawayo, laid out on a grid of wide streets with colonial-era buildings, banks and shops.

    - Compact and walkable during daylight hours
    - Most museums are within walking distance or a short taxi ride

    Two of Bulawayo's oldest townships, with Mzilikazi's art centre and Stanley Square. Best visited with a local guide.

    Suburban shopping centres with supermarkets, banks and cafés, used by locals for everyday shopping.
  </Accordion>

## Food and dining

Bulawayo has a mix of local eateries, hotel restaurants and cafés, mostly in the city centre and the suburban shopping centres. Restaurants open and close often, so check recent reviews or ask your hotel.

**Dining tips:**

- Many restaurants close early in the evening, and some close on Sundays
- Try local dishes such as isitshwala (maize meal) with relishes
- Bulawayo has had long periods of water rationing, so carry bottled water

## Practical information

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How much time to spend" icon="clock">
    - **Minimum:** one to two days for the main city sights
    - **Comfortable:** three days, to include Matobo Hills
    - **Extended:** four to five days, using Bulawayo as a base for the region

    **Suggested plan:**
    - Day 1: city centre, Natural History Museum and Railway Museum
    - Day 2: full day in Matobo Hills
    - Day 3: Khami and the Mzilikazi Art and Craft Centre

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Getting there and around" icon="route">
    **Getting there:**
    - **By air:** Joshua Mqabuko Nkomo International Airport is about 22 km north of the city centre. See our [airport guide](/essentials/airport-guide)
    - **By road:** about 439 km from Harare and 439 km from Victoria Falls. See [travel distances](/essentials/distances)
    - **By bus:** intercity buses run from Harare and Victoria Falls
    - **By rail:** passenger services are limited and often suspended, so check before you plan around them

    **Getting around:**
    - The city centre is walkable by day
    - Taxis are unmetered, so agree the fare first
    - Hire a car or join a tour for Matobo and Khami

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Money" icon="money-bill">
    US dollars are widely used, and the local currency, Zimbabwe Gold (ZiG), also circulates. Bring small US dollar notes and don't rely on ATMs accepting foreign cards. See our [currency and money guide](/essentials/currency-and-money).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Safety and etiquette" icon="shield">
    **Safety tips:**
    - Take normal urban precautions and keep valuables out of sight
    - Avoid isolated areas after dark

    **Cultural notes:**
    - Greet people before getting down to business
    - Ask before photographing people
    - Dress conservatively in official buildings

    See our [travel advice and safety guide](/essentials/travel-advice).

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Day trips from Bulawayo

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Matobo Hills National Park" icon="mountain">
    UNESCO World Heritage site about 35 km south of the city, with granite kopjes, rock art, rhino tracking and World's View.

    - Allow a full day
    - **2026 ZimParks conservation fee:** US$18 international, US$14 SADC, US$4 Zimbabwean for a day visit
    - Take a licensed guide for rhino tracking and rock art

    See our [Matobo Hills guide](/destinations/matobo-hills).

  </Card>

  <Card title="Khami Ruins" icon="monument">
    UNESCO World Heritage site about 22 km west of Bulawayo. Khami was the capital of the Torwa state after Great Zimbabwe declined, from about the mid-15th to the 17th century.

    - Half-day trip from Bulawayo
    - Entry fee set by NMMZ (up to US$15), so check at the site
    - Limited facilities, so bring water

    See our [Khami Ruins guide](/heritage/khami-ruins).

  </Card>

  <Card title="Chipangali Wildlife Orphanage" icon="paw">
    A wildlife orphanage founded in 1973 that cares for injured, seized and orphaned animals. It is about 31 km from the city centre, around 30 minutes' drive, according to Chipangali.

    - Open Tuesday to Sunday, 8:00 to 17:00. Closed on Mondays, and on Christmas Day
    - Entry: US$6 adults, US$5 children under 13

  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Best time to visit

<Card>
  Bulawayo is drier than Harare, with about 574 mm of rain a year. Average figures from the World Meteorological Organization (WMO):

**April to May**

- Average highs of 24 to 26°C, lows of 10 to 13°C
- The rains have usually ended

**June to August (winter)**

- Average highs of 22 to 24°C, lows of about 7 to 9°C
- Almost no rain and clear skies. Bring layers for cold mornings and evenings

**September to October**

- Average highs of 28 to 29°C, the hottest time of year
- Jacarandas flower in the city

**November to March (rainy season)**

- Average highs of 27 to 29°C, with most rain falling in afternoon storms

See [weather by month](/essentials/weather-by-month) for the full table.

**Events:**

- **Zimbabwe International Trade Fair (ZITF):** held in Bulawayo in late April. The 2026 fair ran from 20 to 25 April
- **Intwasa Arts Festival koBulawayo:** held in late September. The 2025 festival ran from 23 to 27 September

</Card>

## Shopping

<Card>
  **Where to buy crafts**

- **Mzilikazi Art and Craft Centre:** pottery, sculpture and other work by its artists
- **Markets and roadside sellers:** crafts across the city (bargaining expected)

**What to look for**

- **Ndebele beadwork:** distinctive geometric patterns
- **Stone sculpture and wood carvings**
- **Basketry**

**Shopping tips**

- Bargaining is expected at markets, not in formal shops
- Check quality carefully
- Zimbabwe protects antiquities, so don't buy items that may be old artefacts

</Card>

<Tip>
  **Bulawayo insider tip:** Start early at the Natural History Museum, walk through Centenary Park into the city centre to see the old buildings around City Hall, then spend the afternoon at Khami, which is quietest late in the day.
</Tip>
