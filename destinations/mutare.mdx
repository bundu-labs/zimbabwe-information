Mutare (formerly Umtali) is one of Zimbabwe's largest cities and the capital of Manicaland Province. It sits in a valley ringed by hills at the foot of the Eastern Highlands, so it combines city services with easy access to some of Zimbabwe's best mountain scenery. Forbes border post into Mozambique is about 8 km from the city centre.

## Top attractions

### Nature and outdoors

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Christmas Pass" icon="road">
    The mountain pass on the main road from Harare, with views over the city and towards Mozambique.

    - Named by pioneers who camped at the foot of the pass on Christmas Day 1890
    - Best in the early morning or late afternoon light
    - Roadside viewpoints

  </Card>

  <Card title="Cecil Kop Nature Reserve" icon="leaf">
    A nature reserve of about 1,500 hectares on the northern edge of the city, set up in 1977 and run by Wildlife and Environment Zimbabwe's Manicaland branch.

    - Three areas: Tiger's Kloof, Thomson's Vlei and a wilderness area
    - Walking trails, game viewing and birding
    - Reached from the city centre via Herbert Chitepo Street and Arcadia Road
    - An entry fee is charged, so check the current rate at reception

  </Card>

  <Card title="Murahwa's Hill" icon="mountain">
    A granite hill on the edge of the city with rock paintings and views over Mutare.

    - A moderate climb
    - Allow two to three hours
    - Check access and any fee locally before you go

  </Card>

  <Card title="Cross Kopje" icon="cross">
    A hill above the city topped by a large cross, with a short hike to the top and views over Mutare. Best in the late afternoon.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Cultural and historical sites

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Mutare Museum" icon="building-columns">
    NMMZ's regional museum for eastern Zimbabwe, with history, natural history and transport collections.

    **Details:**
    - NMMZ lists its museums as open daily, 8:00 to 16:30
    - NMMZ entrance fees range up to US$15, so check the price at the door

  </Card>

  <Card title="Utopia House" icon="landmark">
    A historic house museum with period furnishings and gardens. Check opening times locally before you go.
  </Card>

  <Card title="National Gallery in Mutare" icon="palette">
    The Mutare branch of the National Gallery of Zimbabwe, showing Zimbabwean art. Contact the National Gallery for current exhibitions, hours and admission.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

### Markets and shopping

<Card>
**Sakubva Market:**
Mutare's largest traditional market, in the suburb of Sakubva:
- Fresh produce, household goods and crafts
- Second-hand clothing
- Busy cross-border trade with Mozambique
- Best visited in the morning with a local guide. Keep your belongings close

---

**City centre:**
A compact centre with colonial-era buildings, banks, supermarkets, cafés and craft shops.

</Card>

## Day trips from Mutare

### Bvumba Mountains (about 25 km)

The misty Bvumba (also spelled Vumba) is Mutare's closest mountain escape:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Bvumba Botanical Gardens" icon="flower">
    - Landscaped gardens of indigenous and exotic plants in a mountain setting
    - Walking paths and views towards Mozambique
    - Run by ZimParks

    **2026 ZimParks conservation fee:** US$12 international, US$10 SADC, US$3 Zimbabwean for a day visit

  </Card>

  <Card title="Bunga Forest Botanical Reserve" icon="tree">
    - Indigenous montane forest
    - Good birding
    - Forest walking trails

    Ask ZimParks at the botanical gardens about access and fees.

  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Full Bvumba guide:** [Bvumba Mountains](/destinations/bvumba-mountains)

### Mozambique border trips

Forbes border post is about 8 km from the city centre:

**Day trip options:**

- **Manica:** the closest Mozambican town, a short drive beyond the border
- **Chimoio:** a larger city about 100 km from Mutare

**Practicalities:**

- Check Mozambique's visa rules for your nationality before you go
- Check current border opening hours before you set off
- Carry your passport and vehicle papers

**Beira Corridor:**
The road continues to the port of Beira on the Indian Ocean, about 300 km from Mutare. This is an overland trip that needs planning.

## Food and dining

Mutare has hotel restaurants, cafés and local eateries in the city centre, and lodges in the Bvumba serve meals to non-residents (book ahead). Restaurants open and close often, so check recent reviews.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Local food" icon="utensils">
    **Must try:**
    - Sadza with beef stew
    - Grilled peri-peri chicken
    - Sakubva market food stalls, for the adventurous

  </Card>

  <Card title="Mozambican influence" icon="pepper-hot">
    Cross-border trade brings Mozambican flavours:
    - Prawns and seafood
    - Peri-peri sauces and marinades
    - Portuguese-style bread rolls

  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Practical information

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion icon="clock" title="How much time to spend">
    **Mutare only:**
    - One day for the city sights

    **Mutare as a highland base:**
    - Two to three nights for the city and the Bvumba
    - Four to five nights to add Nyanga or Chimanimani
    - A week for the whole Eastern Highlands

    **Suggested plan:**
    - Day 1: arrive, Christmas Pass at sunset
    - Day 2: Bvumba day trip
    - Day 3: Murahwa's Hill and Cecil Kop, or a Mozambique border trip
    - Day 4 onwards: Nyanga or Chimanimani

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="route" title="Getting there">
    **From Harare:**
    - About 263 km, 3 to 4 hours
    - Route: A3 via Rusape
    - Regular intercity buses

    **From Bulawayo:**
    - About 580 km, 7 to 8 hours
    - Route: via Harare, or via Masvingo and Birchenough Bridge

    **From Mozambique:**
    - Forbes border post is about 8 km east of the city
    - Beira is about 300 km away via Chimoio

    **By air:**
    - Most visitors fly into Harare and drive

    See [travel distances](/essentials/distances).

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="car" title="Getting around">
    **In the city:**
    - Taxis are available. Agree the fare first
    - The centre is walkable by day
    - Kombis (minibuses) for the adventurous

    **To the highlands:**
    - Hire a car for flexibility
    - Some lodges offer transfers

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="money-bill" title="Money">
    - US dollars are widely used
    - ATMs are available, but don't rely on them for foreign cards
    - Cards are accepted at hotels and larger restaurants
    - Carry cash for markets and small vendors
    - Mozambican meticais are useful if you cross the border

    See our [currency and money guide](/essentials/currency-and-money).

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="shield" title="Safety">
    **General:**
    - Take normal urban precautions
    - Avoid walking alone at night in quiet areas
    - Keep your belongings close at Sakubva Market

    **At the border:**
    - Don't carry large amounts of cash where it can be seen
    - Be wary of unofficial "helpers"

    See our [travel advice and safety guide](/essentials/travel-advice).

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion icon="sun" title="Best time to visit">
    **Year-round:** Mutare's altitude keeps temperatures moderate. Average figures from the World Meteorological Organization (WMO):

    - **April to May:** average highs of 24 to 25°C, the end of the rains
    - **June to August:** average highs of 21 to 23°C, lows of about 8 to 9°C. Cooler and often misty in the mountains
    - **September to October:** average highs of 26 to 27°C, clear skies
    - **November to March:** average highs of 26 to 28°C, with most of the year's rain falling in afternoon storms

    See [weather by month](/essentials/weather-by-month).

  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## History of Mutare

<Card>
**Timeline:**

**1890:** Fort Umtali is set up by British South Africa Company pioneers, and pioneers camp at Christmas Pass on Christmas Day

**1891:** The settlement moves to the site now known as Old Mutare, about 14 km north of today's city centre

**1896:** The town moves again, to its current site, to be closer to the new railway from Beira

**After independence:** The city is renamed Mutare

**Today:** The capital of Manicaland and an important commercial centre on the Beira Corridor

</Card>

## Mutare insider tips

<Tip>
**Local knowledge:**
- Christmas Pass is at its best at sunrise
- Friday afternoons are busy at Sakubva as cross-border traders arrive
- The road through the Penhalonga valley is a scenic, slower alternative on the way north
</Tip>

<Warning>
**Important notes:**
- Fill up with fuel in Mutare before heading into remote highland areas
- Mountain weather changes quickly, so bring layers even on warm days
- If you cross into Mozambique, check the visa rules for your nationality first
- Some highland roads need a 4x4 in the rainy season
</Warning>
